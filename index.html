Where exchanges go wrong

The rules are well established. Most problems come down to timing.

01

Day one is your closing day

From the day you close you have 45 calendar days, weekends and holidays included, to name replacement property in writing. Many owners begin the search too late.

02

You can't receive the proceeds

The sale money must go to a qualified intermediary rather than to you. If the exchange isn't arranged before closing, it can't be put right afterwards.

03

Debt counts as well as cash

If your property had a mortgage, you generally need to replace that debt as well as reinvest your equity. Any shortfall can be taxed.

Frequently asked questions

What property qualifies as "like-kind"?

For real estate the definition is broad: most property held for investment or for use in a business can be exchanged for other real property held the same way. A rental home can become an apartment building, land or commercial property. Your personal residence, and property held mainly for resale, don't qualify.

Must I reinvest all of the proceeds?

To defer the full gain you generally need to reinvest all of the net proceeds and replace any debt that was paid off at the sale. Cash you keep, known as "boot", is usually taxable.

My property is already listed or under contract. Is it too late?

Not necessarily. What matters is arranging the exchange with a qualified intermediary before the sale closes. If you're already under contract, it's worth talking it through soon.

What is a Delaware Statutory Trust (DST)?

A DST is a trust that holds investment real estate, such as multifamily, industrial, retail or self-storage property, and lets several investors own fractional interests in it. Interests in a properly structured DST can qualify as like-kind replacement property. DSTs are illiquid securities offered by private placement, typically to accredited investors, and carry risks including loss of value. Offers are made only through the offering documents.

What if my 45 days have already started?

Then naming your replacement property comes first. Some professionally managed options can be identified and closed relatively quickly because the property has already been acquired. A short call can help you see what's realistic in the time remaining.
